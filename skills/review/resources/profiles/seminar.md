# Profile: seminar paper (literature review)

## Goal
An academic seminar paper that presents and analyzes existing research on a defined topic. It is a literature review: it does not report new experiments, it synthesizes and critically evaluates the studies in the project folder.

## Structure (in this order)
The cover page, table of contents and bibliography are generated by the build script. Agents write only the parts below. Headings in the draft use the paper's language; the Hebrew and English forms are given.

1. Abstract / תקציר — one paragraph, 150–250 words: the question, how it was examined (a review of N studies, how they were selected), what was found.
2. Introduction / מבוא — the topic and its relevance, the research question and sub-questions, the scope, and a short roadmap of the paper.
3. Review method / שיטת הסקירה — short (150–400 words): how the sources were chosen, databases, inclusion criteria, time range. Use only what the user supplied in `project.yaml` (`review_method`). If the user supplied nothing, write a minimal factual paragraph (number of sources, years covered, types of studies) and flag it in the notes as "needs the student's input".
4. Content chapters — 2 to 4 chapters, each answering exactly one sub-question. Each chapter ends with a short interim summary that answers its sub-question.
5. Discussion / דיון — compares findings across chapters, cross-cutting insights, conflicts and their likely causes, limitations of the body of research and of this review.
6. Conclusions / מסקנות — a direct answer to the research question and to each sub-question, then directions for future research.

| Part | Share | At 8,000 words |

| Abstract | — | 150–250 |

## Register and depth
- Academic-applied: go into technical detail (methods, datasets, parameters, numbers, formulas) rather than encyclopedia-level explanation.
- Every claim is supported by a source (core rule L2).
- Include formulas, results tables and numeric comparisons where they help. A comparison table should appear in every chapter where at least three studies report comparable numbers.
- Critical analysis is required: what was done, what its limitations are, what does not work, what is missing.
- Matter-of-fact language. No value words such as "amazing", "fascinating", "revolutionary".
- Do not explain basic concepts the academic reader is assumed to know (`assumed_knowledge` in `project.yaml`).

## Critical-analysis checklist (use for each important study)
- Sample size and composition; representativeness
- Design and method; controls; baselines
- Measures and their validity
- Generalizability (setting, population, lab vs. real world)
- Reproducibility (data/code availability, reporting detail)
- Conflicts of interest or funding, when reported
- Whether the conclusions follow from the results

## Do not
- Explain basic concepts the reader is assumed to know.
- Copy paragraphs from sources (core rule L4).
- Write in a popular or journalistic style.
- Include claims without a source.

## Formatting (used by the build script; `project.yaml` → `format` can override)
- Font: David 12pt for Hebrew, Times New Roman 12pt for English
- Line spacing 1.5, margins 2.5 cm, justified text, page numbers centered at the bottom
- Each top-level part starts on a new page
- Tables and figures numbered (Table 1, Table 2 …), number above the table, title on the next line
- Bibliography in APA 7, only works cited in the text, alphabetical
